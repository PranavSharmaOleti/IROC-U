## 1. How to use this tonight

The bank is **234 technical questions with answers**, plus **30 non-technical questions with model
answers**. You cannot memorise 264 answers. So do not try. Use this order instead:

| Stage | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 45 min | Read **Block A** below (10 questions). These are the ones actually traceable to L&T mechanical interviews. If you get nothing else right, get these. |
| 2 | 60 min | Read **Block B** (18 questions). The classic fresher-panel core. |
| 3 | 45 min | Read **Block C** (8 questions). The spine - project, site, safety, why L&T. These decide more than the technical ones. |
| 4 | 60 min | Fill in the final-year project worksheet in `00_START_HERE_TONIGHT.md` section 3, then rehearse Q6, Q7, Q8 of the spine aloud. |
| 5 | 30 min | The mock. Give the interviewer the brief in `00_START_HERE_TONIGHT.md` section 5. |
| 6 | rest of the night | Sweep the remaining bank files for **anything you cannot give a one-line answer to**. Do not read them cover to cover - read the question stems, and only open the answer where you hesitate. |

**How to read an answer:** read the question, answer it out loud from memory first, then open the
answer and see what you missed. Reading an answer without saying it out loud teaches you nothing.

**The three rules that matter more than content:**
1. Answer the question asked, not the one you prepared.
2. Numbers first, then the mechanism, then why it matters in a plant.
3. "I do not know, and here is how I would work it out" beats a confident guess. Every time.

## 8. Two warnings

**Do not read the bank as if it is a listicle of what "they will ask".** It is a syllabus map with
answers. The panel is not running a checklist; it is testing whether you can reason out loud on a
subject you studied two years ago. If you memorise the wording, you will sound rehearsed and you
will fall over on the first follow-up. Memorise the idea, not the sentence.

**Do not pad your answers with things you cannot defend.** Every fact you offer is a handle the
panel can pull. "I know it, but I would check the exact figure in the handbook" is a strong answer
and costs you nothing. A confident wrong number is the only genuinely fatal mistake in an interview
you were otherwise ready for.
