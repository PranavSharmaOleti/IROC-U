## 6. Your BHEL material — condensed crib (the highest-risk area, so keep it sharp)

**Your frame (one line):** *"Plant training attachment at BHEL Hyderabad, Turbines/Compressors/Gas Turbine division. Guided visit with a technical run-down; I was given no design ownership. I took notes on everything I saw."*

**Your three observations (in order of how strong they sound):**
1. **A rotor is machined, not designed.** A large alloy-steel forging goes through a fixed sequence of **19 operations** on the centre lathe and horizontal boring machine; blades are fitted into grooves; shrouding is welded on; the rotor is **balanced stage-wise before the casing closes over it**. The biggest machine in that plant exists for **first-job rotor balancing** — because imbalance force goes as the **square of speed**.
2. **The drawing is only a third of the job.** The **process route** and the **inspection gate** at each operation are the other two-thirds. The casing's horizontal joint is located by **taper pins** and has to be **colour-matched by scraping** before it is accepted.
3. **Scale.** A **12-metre** job machined in **one setup** on a 170 kW heavy-duty CNC lathe — centre height **2100 mm**, **4200 mm** swing without the telescopic cover, and a **4.2 m diameter** job only fits without that cover.

**Specs you'll be asked to confirm** (say "I noted these down" — don't claim design ownership): VMC TK6920, travel 1000×500, spindle dia 200, 350–500 rpm; CNC horizontal lathe centre height 600, centre distance 800, 3 speeds, spindle bore 100; tooling ratio solid:tipped:indexable ≈ **8:4:1**, inserts CNMG/DNMG; journal bearing **Babbitt**, thrust bearing **Kingsbury**; turning gear prevents rotor bowing; labyrinth gland leakage returns to the LP circuit.

**The pivot line:** *"I now think of engineering as a chain from process plan to inspection gate, not as a drawing on a screen — and that's why I want heavy manufacturing rather than design-only work."*
